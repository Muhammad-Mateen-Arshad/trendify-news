University College London Offers Fully-Funded PhD on AI-Based Regional Climate Models

Published on October 09, 2026

Scholarship Overview

University College London (UCL) is offering a fully funded PhD studentship starting in October 2027 through the NERC Centre for Doctoral Training in Understanding Uncertainty to Reduce Climate Risks (UNRISK). The project, titled "Testing the Ability of AI-Based Regional Models to Capture Hydroclimate Changes of the Geologic Past," investigates whether artificial intelligence can enable climate models to accurately recreate historical hydroclimate changes. The successful researcher will be based in UCL Geography’s Ocean, Atmosphere & Cryosphere Dynamics research group under the supervision of Chris Brierley, working alongside project partner National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCAR) and utilizing NVIDIA AI climate technologies.

Who Can Apply

The position is open to both UK and international candidates. Applicants should meet the following criteria:

  • Hold at least a UK 2:1 honours degree or an equivalent international qualification in a relevant academic discipline.
  • Possess a strong quantitative background in fields such as physics, mathematics, Earth sciences, geography, or computer science.
  • Demonstrate basic experience in scientific programming alongside strong verbal and written communication abilities.
  • A Master's degree or previous formal training in climate science or data science is not required, though related experience can strengthen an application.

Benefits

Financial support for this studentship covers a period of three years and nine months and includes:

  • Full payment of tuition fees for both UK and international students.
  • A standard living stipend provided at the UK Research and Innovation rate.
  • A £6,000 Research Training and Support Grant dedicated to research activities.
  • Access to additional funding for cohort-level training, research placements, and specialized instruction.
  • Note: Funding does not cover UK visa fees or the Immigration Health Surcharge.

How to Apply

Candidates must contact the project supervisor prior to submitting an official application. Applicants can choose up to two research projects through UNRISK, but must contact at least one supervisor per project. Submission requires completing university and UNRISK application forms with comprehensive details regarding academic background, training, work experience, and research history.

Applications close at 1:00 PM GMT on January 13, 2027. Interviews are scheduled for March 2027, and admission offers will be issued later that month.
