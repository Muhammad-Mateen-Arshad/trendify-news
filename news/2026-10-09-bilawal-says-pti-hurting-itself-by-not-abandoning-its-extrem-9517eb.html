Bilawal says PTI hurting itself by not abandoning its 'extremist politics', urges party to change its strategy

Published on October 09, 2026

According to Dawn, Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari has urged the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) to alter its political strategy and abandon what he described as "extremist politics." Speaking in Karachi, Bilawal stated that the party's current choices are damaging its own political standing, disappointing its supporters, and harming the nation.

What happened

During his speech, Bilawal called on the PTI to abandon confrontational tactics and participate in mainstream political dialogue. His comments come as the PTI moves forward with a long march to demand the release of its jailed founder and former Prime Minister Imran Khan, despite warnings from federal authorities against holding gatherings in Islamabad. (The specific title and organizer of the Karachi event were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.)

Key facts

  • Bilawal warned that the PTI risks losing its government in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa if it maintains its current strategy, noting that it holds no political role in Karachi or the federal government.
  • He stated that the federal government was prepared to impose an emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa a week earlier, describing such a measure as a constitutional last resort.
  • According to Dawn, Bilawal criticized the Chief Minister of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa for rejecting dialogue in favor of negative politics after political parties had reached an agreement.
  • He pointed out that the PTI is confronting Parliament, the judiciary, the federal government, the establishment, and other parties, while avoiding parliamentary work like the electoral reforms committee.
  • Bilawal remarked that persistent confrontation could cause Imran Khan to stay in prison longer than President Asif Ali Zardari, who currently holds the record for the longest imprisonment.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Bilawal emphasized that political stability and cooperation between political parties are urgently required to resolve the country's economic difficulties. Citing figures from the World Bank, he noted that Pakistan carries the highest regional burden of poverty and unemployment. He stated that if the PTI moves away from extreme tactics, opportunities for dialogue will open up to address its political concerns and help deliver public relief.
