Somali forces recover oil tanker carrying Pakistani crewmen over 5 months after hijacking by pirates

Published by AI Pipeline | September 29, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Somali maritime authorities recovered the MT Honour 25, a Palau-flagged oil tanker hijacked by armed pirates off the coast of Puntland on April 21. The Puntland Maritime Police Force executed a planned operation using land and sea units in the Dharin-Bar area of the Bandar Beyla district. Authorities confirmed that the operation concluded with the ship safely brought under the control of the Puntland government. During the action, security forces apprehended 21 suspected pirates, and efforts remain ongoing to locate additional suspects. State television in Puntland reported that those arrested will be presented in court.

Key facts

The vessel carried a 19-member crew, comprising 10 Pakistanis, seven Indonesians, one Indian, and one Sri Lankan. Initial reports attributed to the crew indicate that five people died and four were injured during the captivity. Further official details regarding the operation and full casualty lists are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

  • The tanker was held for more than five months without progress in negotiations or the payment of a reported $3 million ransom.
  • Resolving the situation was stalled by an ownership dispute, as the individual previously identified as the ship's owner was likely an intermediary.
  • While held hostage, the crew released video and audio appeals stating that clean water, food, and medicine had run out.
  • The Ansar Burney Trust reported that the International Maritime Organisation promised to cooperate to help return the injured sailors and the deceased to their home countries.

Why it matters

The hijacking highlights a recent rise in Somali piracy. Dawn reported that at least 15 attacks have occurred since April, representing the highest number recorded since 2013. The months-long ordeal prompted international appeals from the hostages' families to global bodies, including the United Nations and the International Maritime Organisation, alongside diplomatic engagement by Pakistani officials seeking intervention for their citizens.
