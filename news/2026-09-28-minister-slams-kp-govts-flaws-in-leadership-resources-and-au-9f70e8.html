Minister slams KP govt’s ‘flaws’ in leadership, resources and authority in counter-terror fight

Published by AI Pipeline | September 28, 2026

Federal ministers have sharply criticized the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) leadership governing Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP), accusing the provincial administration of failing to adequately manage counter-terrorism efforts, according to a report by Dawn. Speaking at a press conference in Islamabad, Minister of State for Interior Tallal Chaudhry and Information Minister Attaullah Tarar asserted that the KP government has provided insufficient leadership, equipment, and funding to security forces.

What happened

According to Dawn, Chaudhry stated that although the PTI has governed the province since 2013, its administration has created operational space for militant groups rather than strengthening law enforcement. He criticized the provincial counter-terrorism setup, noting that the Counter-Terrorism Department (CTD) is not fully functional throughout the province. Chaudhry further stated that local forces lack basic equipment, bulletproof jackets, specialized training, forensic labs, and Safe City projects. He also questioned how the provincial government utilized its Rs800 billion allocation from the National Finance Commission (NFC) Award.

Key facts

  • Leadership criticism: Chaudhry noted that KP Chief Minister Sohail Afridi skipped funeral services for victims of the September 18 Kohat blast and failed to attend federal counter-terrorism meetings despite requesting funds.
  • Vehicle registration delays: Tarar highlighted that KP has not mandated full vehicle registration, which he stated hampers efforts to track and survey militants.
  • Cross-border issues: Chaudhry criticized KP leadership for failing to condemn cross-border terrorism from Afghanistan following a recent suicide attack in Dera Ismail Khan. Dawn reported that Pakistan conducted strikes across 10 locations in Afghanistan and issued a demarche to Afghan authorities after a September 23 airspace violation by drones.
  • Intelligence usage: The interior official stated that authorities used open-source intelligence to geolocate a suspect involved in an attack on a rescue vehicle to Pashtunistan Square in Kabul.

Why it matters

The statements reflect escalating political friction between federal authorities and the KP provincial administration over regional security management. According to Dawn, the ministers warned that legislative actions, including proposed revisions to the KP Police Act, risk reducing the authority of law enforcement officials and weakening coordinated national counter-terrorism operations.
