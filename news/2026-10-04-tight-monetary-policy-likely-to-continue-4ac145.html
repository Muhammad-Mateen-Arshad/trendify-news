Tight monetary policy likely to continue

Published by AI Pipeline | October 04, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, financial analysts expect the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) to maintain its tight monetary policy stance rather than lowering interest rates to stimulate growth. Global oil price volatility and persistent cost-push inflation mean the central bank is unlikely to hit its FY27 mid-term inflation target of 5 to 7 percent. Instead, officials are maintaining focus on limited but sustainable economic growth.

Key facts

Key information reported by Dawn includes:

  • Consumer Price Index inflation reached 10.3 percent in September.
  • The SBP policy rate currently stands at 11.5 percent, though analysts disagree on whether it will remain unchanged.
  • Tresmark Chief Executive Faisal Mamsa noted that authorities project inflation to average 7.5 percent if oil trends toward $80 per barrel, or 8.2 percent if oil stays near $100 per barrel, with moderation expected after December.
  • At a Wednesday T-bill auction, the government raised cut-off yields by up to 75 basis points, reaching 12.49 percent for one-year paper, hinting at a potential rate hike at the upcoming Monetary Policy Committee meeting on October 26.
  • Industrial energy costs in Pakistan run between 14 and 16 cents per unit, compared to 7 to 8 cents in Bangladesh.
  • National economic growth has remained between 3 and 3.7 percent for four years, failing to create jobs or address poverty affecting 44 percent of Pakistanis.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, ongoing double-digit inflation alongside high energy costs is heavily burdening local industries, investors, and the public. Business figures, including textile manufacturer Amir Aziz and industry executive Mohammad Hasham, expressed concern that high operating expenses make Pakistani products less competitive compared to regional peers like Bangladesh. Additionally, while local authorities aim to maintain a stable rupee to suppress imported inflation, the International Monetary Fund continues to press for a market-based exchange rate.
