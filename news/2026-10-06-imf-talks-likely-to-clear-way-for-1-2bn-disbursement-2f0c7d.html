IMF talks likely to clear way for $1.2bn disbursement

Published on October 06, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, talks between Pakistani authorities and a visiting International Monetary Fund (IMF) staff mission are expected to conclude positively, clearing the way for a $1.2 billion disbursement. This funding is part of two concurrent initiatives: a $7 billion Extended Fund Facility and a $1.4 billion Resilience and Sustainability Facility. Both sides are currently finalizing the Memorandum of Economic and Fiscal Policies following a biannual review. The exact date for the IMF Executive Board meeting was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • Mission details: The IMF delegation, led by Ms Iva Petrova, did not introduce new demands beyond adjustments for previous target slippages. Revenue targets remain intact due to strong first-quarter collections.
  • LNG imports and energy: Gas companies requested 22 LNG cargoes for the winter season amid supply challenges tied to the US-Iran conflict. However, an energy task force promised 10 to 12 cargoes, and sources suggest actual imports may only reach seven or eight.
  • Financial considerations: Each spot LNG cargo costs approximately $100 million, directly impacting current account targets agreed with the IMF. The import plan requires approval from the Prime Minister, the Finance Ministry, and the State Bank of Pakistan.
  • Gas sector debt: Circular debt in the gas sector has expanded to Rs3.6 trillion, consisting of roughly Rs1.8 trillion in principal payables and an equal amount in accrued interest and fees.
  • Subsidy reform plan: Pakistan committed to transitioning from general cross-subsidies to targeted subsidies via the Benazir Income Support Programme by January 2027, supported by World Bank registry integration scheduled for late November.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, successfully completing this review is critical for Pakistan to secure vital foreign financing while balancing strict macroeconomic targets. Managing winter LNG import costs and addressing rising circular debt are key elements in maintaining economic stability and meeting commitments made to international lenders.
