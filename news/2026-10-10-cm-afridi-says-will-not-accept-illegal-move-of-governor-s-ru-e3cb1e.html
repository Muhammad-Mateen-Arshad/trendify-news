CM Afridi says will not accept 'illegal move' of governor's rule or emergency in KP

Published on October 10, 2026

According to Dawn, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) Chief Minister Sohail Afridi has stated that he will not accept any illegal measures, such as the imposition of emergency or governor’s rule in the province. Speaking during a Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) protest march, Afridi warned that if the federal government takes such decisions, the protest rally will alter its route toward Peshawar before advancing to D-Chowk in Islamabad. The federal administration is reportedly weighing governor's rule or an emergency due to disputes over counterterrorism strategy.

Key facts

Dawn reported the following details regarding the ongoing protest and provincial situation:

  • The PTI long march began on Oct 4 to demand the release of jailed party founder Imran Khan, following the failure of Oct 3 negotiations with the federal government.
  • Afridi asserted that he has repeatedly rejected pressure to sign off on military operations in the province or compromise on his security stance.
  • The march reached Mardan after passing through Charsadda, with regional convoys planned to gather at the Swabi Interchange between Oct 12 and Oct 14.
  • Afridi is expected to disclose the next phase of the demonstration on Oct 13 at the Swabi Interchange.
  • Shipping containers have been positioned near the Swabi Interchange and the Indus River bridge on the M-1 Motorway to impede the march, though traffic on the motorway and Grand Trunk Road remains open.
  • KP Inspector General Zulfiqar Hameed issued police operational instructions regarding protest management. Specific details of these instructions were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the standoff reflects deepening tension between the federal leadership and the PTI-led government in KP. Afridi, who was elected chief minister in October 2025, has persistently opposed federal policies concerning counterterrorism and Afghanistan. The party maintains that it will continue its demonstrations despite warnings from the federal government and potential legal or executive interventions in the province.
