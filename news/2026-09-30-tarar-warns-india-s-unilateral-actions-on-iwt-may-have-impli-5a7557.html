Tarar warns India's unilateral actions on IWT may have implications for water governance globally

Published by AI Pipeline | September 30, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Pakistan’s Information Minister Attahullah Tarar warned that India’s unilateral decision to hold the Indus Waters Treaty (IWT) in abeyance creates a dangerous precedent for international water governance. Speaking at an event in Islamabad, Tarar urged the international community to take note of India's actions, asserting that no nation should be allowed to weaponize water or arbitrarily suspend a binding international accord.

Key facts

Dawn outlined several essential details regarding the ongoing dispute between the two countries:

  • India unilaterally put the treaty in abeyance in April of last year, which was followed by a short military confrontation in May 2025.
  • Pakistan rejected India's action as an violation of the 1969 Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties and stated that any attempt to block its water share is an act of war.
  • An August ruling by the Permanent Court of Arbitration supported Pakistan's position, declaring the treaty fully in force and finding no legal basis for India to alter or suspend it.
  • The 1960 accord assigns the eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, and Sutlej) to India, while the western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, and Chenab) are largely assigned to Pakistan.
  • Addressing India's justification regarding cross-border terrorism, Tarar pointed out that Pakistan has suffered over $50 billion in economic losses while actively fighting terrorism.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, Tarar emphasized that the Indus waters represent a basic right for Pakistan's 240 million people and serve as a vital source of regional stability. He warned that if a nation can unilaterally ignore established dispute resolution frameworks to suspend a water-sharing agreement, it threatens transboundary water management worldwide. Pakistan has maintained a diplomatic effort to protect its rights, with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif telling the United Nations General Assembly that any attempt to usurp the country's water rights would be treated as an act of war.
