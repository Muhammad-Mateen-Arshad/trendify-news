Afridi invites people from across country to join PTI's long march at Swabi Interchange on Oct 11

Published on October 09, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) Chief Minister Sohail Afridi has invited citizens from across Pakistan to join the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) long march at the Swabi Interchange on Oct 11. The demonstration, which began on Oct 4 to demand the release of jailed party founder Imran Khan, has so far stayed within KP. The final destination beyond Swabi remains tentative, as the march's route plan has undergone several adjustments since its launch.

Key facts

  • The PTI march was launched following failed negotiations with the federal government, particularly regarding differences over counterterrorism policy, according to Dawn.
  • CM Afridi outlined plans for regional convoys from Peshawar, Malakand, Kohat, Bannu, and Dera Ismail Khan to head toward Swabi or travel along the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor route.
  • Afridi criticized the federal government's economic and security record since 2022, stating that 14,000 intelligence-based operations and 22 major military operations between 2004 and 2026 had failed to eliminate terrorism in KP.
  • KP Governor Faisal Karim Kundi challenged PTI protesters to actually reach and cross Swabi, accusing party leaders of hiding and taking repeated plan revisions.
  • Governor Kundi called on the KP cabinet to resign over local strikes and security concerns, noting that the federal leadership could consider an emergency or governor's rule if the provincial situation worsens.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the long march highlights worsening political and administrative friction between the federal government and KP's provincial leadership. Federal ministers have warned the PTI against extending the march beyond KP, while broader policy disagreements over counterterrorism and central funding continue to fuel political instability between the Centre and the province.
