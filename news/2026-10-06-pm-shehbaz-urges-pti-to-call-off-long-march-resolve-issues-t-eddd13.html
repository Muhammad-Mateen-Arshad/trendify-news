PM Shehbaz urges PTI to call off long march, resolve issues through dialogue

Published on October 06, 2026

What happened

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has urged the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) to stop its long march toward Islamabad and resolve political disputes through dialogue, according to Dawn. Speaking at a groundbreaking ceremony for the Kashmir Chowk underpass in Islamabad, the premier noted that two days of talks in National Assembly Speaker Ayaz Sadiq’s chamber between PTI and government representatives failed to resolve the standoff. The opposition proceeded with its march to demand the release of jailed party founder Imran Khan, despite explicit warnings from federal authorities that gatherings in the capital would be prevented.

Key facts

  • Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif stated that the PML-N and PPP attempted to engage PTI in negotiations, but claimed the opposition sought to alter previously settled points to ensure the talks failed.
  • Minister of State for Interior Tallal Chaudhry held Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi responsible for the breakdown in discussions, noting that PTI negotiators withdrew after consulting Afridi at the last minute.
  • Chaudhry stated that both sides had previously reached a initial draft agreement agreeing on counterterrorism measures.
  • Resumption of negotiations remains conditional on PTI calling off its demonstrations, with Chaudhry adding that political and constitutional measures are prepared if protests continue.
  • Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi remarked that citizens should evaluate which public representatives are focused on welfare versus those participating in negative politics.

Why it matters

According to Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, continuous political confrontational tactics and street protests directly damage the national economy by disrupting manufacturing, imports, and exports. Sharif highlighted that macroeconomic stability had been restored over the past 2.5 years, citing gains in September regarding remittances, production, and information technology exports. The premier warned that harming economic stability or endangering public safety would not be permitted and that the law would take its course.
