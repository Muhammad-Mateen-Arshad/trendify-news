Pakistan, Turkiye, Saudi Arabia agree to rapid force deployment in the kingdom under Makkah pact

Published on October 05, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, Pakistan, Turkiye, and Saudi Arabia have agreed to execute practical measures for the rapid deployment of military forces and capabilities within Saudi Arabia. The agreement was reached during an emergency meeting of the Strategic Political and Defence Committee (SPDC) under the Makkah Defence Alliance in Riyadh, convened to address escalating security threats targeting the kingdom.

Key facts

  • The alliance agreed to move forward with collective defence commitments to enhance joint military integration and collective deterrence against security threats.
  • The exact size and timeline of the forces being deployed were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • The committee condemned ongoing attacks against Saudi infrastructure, cities, and attempted targeting of Makkah and Madina.
  • Retired Pakistani Lieutenant General Nauman Mahmood was named as the first secretary general of the alliance, serving a three-year term.
  • Pakistan's representation at the meeting included Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar, Chief of Army Staff Field Marshal Asim Munir, and Defence Minister Khawaja Asif.
  • Defence Minister Khawaja Asif noted that Saudi forces are conducting combat operations, while Pakistani and Turkish militaries are currently providing supportive assistance, such as reconnaissance.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the emergency session follows persistent strikes by Yemen's Houthi forces against Saudi facilities, alongside broader regional conflict. Signed on August 7, the Makkah pact commits the three member nations to mutual defence, treating an assault on one as an attack on all. The Riyadh meeting formalised the alliance's organizational structure, implementing previous pledges by Pakistani officials to back Saudi security and territorial integrity.
