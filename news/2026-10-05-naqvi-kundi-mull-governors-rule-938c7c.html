Naqvi, Kundi mull governor’s rule

Published on October 05, 2026

What happened

Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi met with Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) Governor Faisal Karim Kundi on Sunday to review the regional political and security situation, according to Dawn. The meeting occurred amid growing speculation regarding the potential imposition of governor's rule in the province. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Inspector General of Police Zulfikar Hameed was also present during the talks, which centered on addressing unrest and managing security challenges following a political march toward Islamabad.

Key facts

  • According to an official Governor House statement, both leaders voiced strong concern that terrorists might safely pass through security checkpoints during the march organized by the PTI.
  • The officials agreed that the Governor's House will fulfill its complete constitutional duties if the president and prime minister decide that governor's rule or a state of emergency is necessary to protect the province from violence and destruction.
  • The Governor House statement characterized the provincial government's stance during discussions on counterterrorism measures as an attitude opposing the interests of the local population and the province.
  • Naqvi and Kundi stated that utilizing provincial helicopters and other state resources for the march by Afridi and provincial ministers constituted a clear violation of an Islamabad High Court directive.
  • An unnamed government source cited by Dawn revealed that federal leadership is actively weighing governor's rule, adding that an emergency declaration could be imminent following the PTI's exit from counterterrorism negotiations.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, national and provincial authorities are debating major administrative measures to tackle rising security threats and political friction in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Governor Kundi stated that protecting citizens remains the top priority and called on all state bodies to work jointly to overcome terrorism. Meanwhile, federal officials maintain that their demands for provincial assistance in counterterrorism efforts are reasonable and necessary. Specific operational steps or dates regarding any potential administrative takeover were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
