‘Calibrated’ air strikes target terrorist hideouts at two locations in Afghanistan: info ministry

Published by AI Pipeline | October 01, 2026

Pakistan carried out targeted air strikes against terrorist hideouts at two sites in Afghanistan on Thursday, according to a report by Dawn citing the country's information ministry. The action was undertaken as part of Operation Ghazab lil Haq to dismantle militant networks and protect citizens. The ministry reported that initial findings indicate 22 terrorists were killed, while significant stockpiles of weapons and ammunition were destroyed.

What happened

The aerial operations selectively focused on camps belonging to organizations referred to by state authorities as Fitna al Khawarij—which denotes the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP)—and Fitna al Hindustan, a term used for designated Balochistan-based groups. Officials stated that the strikes relied on credible intelligence and were executed with care to prevent collateral damage. Key facts

  • The strikes were conducted under Operation Ghazab lil Haq, an initiative launched on February 26 after cross-border shelling by the Afghan Taliban.
  • The latest action follows earlier September military engagements, including strikes on September 21 that resulted in more than 28 militant deaths.
  • Pakistan previously downed eight cross-border drones, conducted precision strikes across 10 Afghan locations, and delivered a formal demarche on September 26 regarding airspace violations.
  • A UN Security Council monitoring report covering December 16, 2025, to June 9, 2026, indicated that support for the TTP from the Afghan Taliban has heightened the frequency and severity of attacks in Pakistan.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, diplomatic relations between Pakistan and Afghanistan remain strained over cross-border security concerns. Pakistani officials have repeatedly called on Kabul to dismantle terrorist sanctuaries on Afghan soil. The UN report noted increasing operational links among the TTP, Al Qaeda, Islamic State-Khorasan, and the Baloch Liberation Army, underscoring ongoing regional instability.
