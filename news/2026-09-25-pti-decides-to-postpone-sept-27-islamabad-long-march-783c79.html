PTI decides to postpone Sept 27 Islamabad long march

Published by AI Pipeline | September 25, 2026

What happened

The leadership of Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) decided on Friday to delay its planned long march to Islamabad by more than a week, according to Dawn. Originally scheduled to begin on September 27, the demonstration is now set to depart from Peshawar on October 4. The party is organizing the nationwide protest to press for the release of its founder, Imran Khan, who is currently incarcerated in Rawalpindi’s Adiala jail.

Key facts

According to Dawn, several administrative and political developments surround the postponed demonstration:

  • Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi is expected to formally announce the updated schedule soon.
  • An unnamed senior PTI official stated that the postponement was intended to exhaust law enforcement agencies and the federal government, which have already restricted movement and spent substantial amounts on security preparations.
  • The PTI official confirmed that the decision was independent of Jamaat-i-Islami's (JI) postponed protest regarding the petroleum levy.
  • Section 144 has been enforced in Islamabad for two months, while Punjab has implemented Section 144 statewide for 13 days due to security concerns.
  • The Punjab government requested Rangers deployment in Rawalpindi, Attock, Jhelum, and Mianwali from September 22 to October 5.
  • Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi had earlier cautioned PTI against proceeding with the protest, and three Pakistan Army companies were previously deployed in Rawalpindi in August for six months.

Why it matters

According to a senior PTI leader cited by Dawn, the party believes it has achieved its immediate goal by causing the government to spend heavily on security forces and block major routes. Meanwhile, law enforcement agencies and local administrations in Rawalpindi and Islamabad remain on high alert to maintain public order during potential demonstrations.
