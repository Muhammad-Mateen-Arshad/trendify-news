Govt assails PTI over 'lack of governance' in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

Published on October 10, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, Federal Information Minister Attaullah Tarar and Coordinator to the Prime Minister on Information for Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Affairs Ikhtiar Wali Khan criticized the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) provincial government over claims of misgovernance in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). Speaking at a press conference in Islamabad, federal representatives accused the provincial administration of neglecting public infrastructure, health, and security while misusing development funds for political protests.

Key facts

  • According to Dawn, Tarar alleged that the KP government received Rs700 billion under the National Finance Commission for provincial development, but diverted these resources to organize protests instead of upgrading the Counter Terrorism Department, building forensic labs, or installing safe city cameras.
  • Tarar claimed the PTI administration has set the province back over the last 13 and a half years, failing to build major hospitals, highways, or universities, and accused the party of acting as a facilitator for terrorism in the region.
  • Ikhtiar Wali Khan stated that 18 out of 34 universities in KP are facing potential bankruptcy due to leadership shortages. He also accused provincial ministers of corruption and claimed that funds intended for counterterrorism efforts were withheld from local police forces.
  • Asked about the potential implementation of emergency rule or governor's rule in KP, Tarar stated that responding to the question was premature, though he asserted that conditions in the province were severe.
  • A direct response from the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa provincial leadership regarding these claims was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the statements highlight deepening political conflict between the PML-N-led federal government and the PTI-led provincial government in KP. Friction over counterterrorism policies led to the collapse of political talks on October 3, and federal ministers have linked ongoing security concerns and PTI-led protest marches to discussions regarding potential emergency measures or governor's rule in the province.
