Naqvi accuses KP govt of backing out of counterterrorism commitments during Centre-opposition talks

Published by AI Pipeline | October 04, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, weekend negotiations between the federal government and opposition representatives at Parliament House ended without a breakthrough. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi stated that the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) government withdrew from agreed counterterrorism commitments during the final phase of talks. The discussions began after 4pm on Saturday to address the Pakistan Tehreek-i-Insaf (PTI) planned march to Islamabad on October 4, but extended into early Sunday morning without resolving key disputes.

Key facts

According to Dawn, statements from government officials and opposition leaders outlined the following points regarding the failed negotiations:

  • Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi stated that the KP government initially agreed to submit a counterterrorism implementation plan within 72 hours. The draft commitments included reviewing the Police Act 2026, explicitly condemning the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan, supporting Afghan refugee repatriation, avoiding contact with the interim Afghan government, and implementing the National Action Plan.
  • Minister of State for Interior Tallal Chaudhry claimed the talks failed primarily because the KP government would not assure future cooperation against terrorism.
  • Senate Opposition Leader Allama Raja Nasir Abbas stated that disagreements over terrorism policy in KP and medical access for jailed PTI founder Imran Khan caused the deadlock.
  • KP Chief Minister Sohail Afridi shared a proposed draft on X, asserting that its demands were fully constitutional and legal.
  • The KP draft requested joint political committees, medical access and visits for Imran Khan and Bushra Bibi by personal doctors, the swift release of female political prisoners including Yasmin Rashid, judicial consideration of petitions within 10 days where legally possible, and federal-provincial security strategy meetings within 72 hours.
  • The draft stated that PTI would cancel its long march upon receiving written guarantees from the federal government regarding these terms.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the division reflects significant disagreement between federal authorities and the KP provincial administration over counterterrorism coordination and the handling of jailed political leaders. Interior Minister Naqvi emphasized that security matters should not be politicized while the province faces ongoing security threats. Details regarding future negotiation schedules are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
