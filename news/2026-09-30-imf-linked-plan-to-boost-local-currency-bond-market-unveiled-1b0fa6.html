IMF-linked plan to boost local currency bond market unveiled

Published by AI Pipeline | September 30, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, the Pakistani government has announced a strategic action plan for the Local Currency Bond Market (LCBM) that allows the general public to trade government securities, including treasury bills and bonds, on the stock market. This step is intended to advance compliance under an International Monetary Fund (IMF) programme.

The announcement followed an opening meeting between Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb and an IMF delegation led by Iva Petrova. The mission is conducting a review for the release of roughly $1.2 billion, split into $1 billion under the Extended Fund Facility and $200 million under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility. Successful completion of talks could lead to funds being disbursed by late October or early November, though the country may require executive board waivers for structural benchmark slippages. Details regarding the exact benchmark slippages are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • The LCBM action plan was required under the IMF agreement by September 30 and builds on a joint IMF-World Bank diagnostic study.
  • Commercial banks currently control 78 percent of government securities, while sovereign debt makes up about 62 percent of total banking assets.
  • Eligible bank customers will be allowed to trade listed government debt through their banks under supervision from the State Bank of Pakistan, the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan, the Pakistan Stock Exchange, and the Central Depository Company.
  • The plan outlines a securities-lending facility for primary dealers and mandates a review of the primary dealer framework for fiscal year 2027-28 using E-Bond secondary-market performance.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the initiative addresses a key weakness in the market by broadening the investor base across retail, institutional, and international buyers. The plan sets out five main objectives: boosting institutional capacity, establishing direct accountability, improving primary issuance predictability, building secondary-market liquidity, and modernizing market infrastructure while eliminating legal and tax hurdles. These measures aim to lower medium-term government funding costs, strengthen monetary policy implementation, and establish a reliable yield curve for private-sector financing.
