Australia probes flydubai co-pilot's links to the country, police say

Published on October 04, 2026

What happened

An Australian counterterrorism team has launched an investigation into the ties an airline co-pilot has to Australia following an alleged attack and hijacking attempt aboard a flight bound for Israel, according to a report by Dawn. Authorities state that the co-pilot attacked the captain with an emergency crash axe during flydubai flight FZ1073 from Dubai. The assault resulted in the aircraft plunging 16,000 feet in about 30 seconds before crew members, passengers, and an off-duty reserve pilot managed to subdue the suspect. The flight was safely landed in Saudi Arabia, where the individual was taken into custody before being transferred to the United Arab Emirates.

Key facts

  • The Victorian Joint Counter Terrorism Team—comprising federal police, Victorian state police, and intelligence agency ASIO—is examining the suspect's history in Australia.
  • Sources state the suspect is an Omani national named Hamam Al-Hamami who studied at RMIT University in Melbourne roughly a decade ago.
  • The UAE prosecutor general designated the assault on the aircraft, which carried over 170 passengers and crew, as a terrorist attack.
  • Media reports indicate Australian intelligence previously held a file on the individual following visits to mosques and extremist websites during his time in Australia.
  • Additional details regarding formal charges against the suspect are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why it matters

The event has prompted intense security concerns and political fallout in Israel. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered a formal review of foreign civil aviation security protocols. Questions have been raised regarding how a pilot from Oman—a nation that lacks official diplomatic ties with Israel—was allowed to operate a flight heading to Tel Aviv. Israeli opposition figures have criticized the official response, while debates persist over whether foreign airline security falls under the responsibility of domestic or foreign intelligence agencies.
