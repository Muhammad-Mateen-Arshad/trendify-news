Kyiv bridge hit in further Russian drone attack as German chancellor makes surprise visit

Published on October 04, 2026

What happened

Russian drones have targeted key bridges in Kyiv for four consecutive days as Moscow escalates strikes on critical infrastructure in Ukraine's capital, according to BBC News. On Sunday morning, a drone crashed into and exploded on the Pivnichnyi (Northern) Bridge for the second day in a row, injuring two people and leaving a hole in a traffic lane. The Pivdennyi (Southern) Bridge was also hit several times earlier in the week, resulting in damaged cables.

The attacks occurred alongside a surprise visit to Kyiv by German Chancellor Friedrich Merz. Meanwhile, additional Russian strikes took place in other regions: a drone attack on a warehouse in Sumy killed one person, while a strike on a communications facility in Dnipro injured six people. Ukraine's Air Force reported shooting down 125 out of 135 drones launched by Russia overnight.

Key facts

  • Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko confirmed two injuries from the latest bridge strike and noted that a separate drone attack damaged a heating main, causing temporary hot water shortages in the Desnyanskyi district.
  • While the struck bridges remain standing, damage and transit restrictions have caused heavy gridlock. Local officials are planning pontoon bridges, ferry services, and a river tunnel to maintain crossing options.
  • During his visit, Chancellor Merz reaffirmed Germany's support. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky announced a new German aid package worth over €1.3bn ($1.5bn) aimed at providing weapons and supporting the energy sector.
  • Russia's defence ministry announced it is increasing strikes across Ukraine, claiming it targeted bridges used for troop movements in response to Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries.
  • Russia's foreign ministry reiterated warnings for foreign diplomats to evacuate Kyiv, but EU Ambassador Katarína Mathernová and German Ambassador Boris Ruge stated their delegations will remain in the city.

Why it matters

Kyiv's eight bridges across the Dnipro River are vital for the capital's estimated three million residents, serving approximately 500,000 vehicles every day, according to BBC News. Experts explained that while drone strikes are unlikely to completely collapse the bridges, continued targeting could weaken their structures and force long-term closures. Furthermore, Germany has become Ukraine's largest individual donor since Merz assumed office in May 2025, with Merz stating that European security directly depends on the outcome of the conflict.
