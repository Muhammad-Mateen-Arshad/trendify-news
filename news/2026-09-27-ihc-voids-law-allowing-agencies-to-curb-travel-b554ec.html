IHC voids law allowing agencies to curb travel

Published by AI Pipeline | September 27, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, the Islamabad High Court (IHC) ruled that a provision within the Passport Rules, 2021, which allowed government agencies to restrict citizens from traveling abroad, is unconstitutional. Justice Raja Inaam Ameen Minhas invalidated the second limb of Rule 22(2)(b), declaring it contrary to the Passports Act, 1974, as well as Articles 4, 9, 10A, and 15 of the Constitution. The court held that placing individuals on the Passport Control List (PCL) based on recommendations from government departments without a named competent authority or basic legal safeguards is of no legal effect.

Key facts

  • The decision followed a petition by Moinuddin, who was prevented from boarding a flight to Saudi Arabia for Umrah at Islamabad International Airport on Dec 18, 2025, despite having a valid visa, ticket, and passport.
  • His name was put on the PCL on Dec 24, 2024, following a request from NCB-INTERPOL submitted through the Assistant Director of the FIA Immigration Wing.
  • The request stemmed from Moinuddin's prior conviction and three-year prison sentence in the UK for causing death by dangerous driving while under the influence, after which he was deported.
  • The IHC observed that Moinuddin had completed his sentence, had no pending criminal cases or investigations in Pakistan, was not an extraditable subject, and had never had his passport cancelled or impounded.
  • The court highlighted that the struck-down rule lacked clear criteria, mandatory notices, hearings, or an identified decision-making authority, leaving citizens unaware of travel bans until stopped at immigration counters.
  • The court invalidated an agency order from Jan 26, 2026, declared Moinuddin's PCL listing unlawful, and directed officials to remove his name within 15 days of receiving a certified copy of the judgment.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the judgment clarifies that administrative bodies lack the power to restrain travel unless explicitly granted by law following due process. The court ruled that vague justifications such as protecting national reputation or creating deterrence do not grant jurisdiction when no active legal necessity exists. Compliance directives were issued to the Interior Secretary and the Directors General of both the FIA and Immigration and Passports.
