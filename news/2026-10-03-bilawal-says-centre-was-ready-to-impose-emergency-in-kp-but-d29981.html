Bilawal says Centre was ready to impose emergency in KP but PPP sought 'political solution'

Published by AI Pipeline | October 03, 2026

What happened

According to a report by Dawn, Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) Chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari stated that the federal government was fully prepared to impose an emergency or governor’s rule in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). Speaking at an event in Karachi, Bilawal explained that the PPP advised the government to seek a political solution instead to address growing counter-terrorism concerns. He urged political parties to hold talks with the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) regarding its scheduled October 4 protest march to Islamabad. During the same event, Bilawal also announced several water infrastructure projects designed to ease supply shortages in Karachi.

Key facts

  • According to Dawn, Bilawal noted that federal officials considered constitutional emergency measures in KP due to persistent terrorist threats emanating from Afghanistan and external support from India.
  • The PPP chairman urged political leaders to engage in dialogue with PTI before taking emergency steps, while calling on PTI to cancel its planned October 4 march.
  • Bilawal stated that governor’s rule or emergency powers could still be invoked if the KP provincial government fails to address law and order or cooperate with federal authorities.
  • He proposed establishing a formal written agreement between the federal government and KP outlining a shared strategy to fight terrorism, keeping security matters separate from political deadlocks.
  • Following a meeting between Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and President Asif Ali Zardari, a joint delegation of PML-N and PPP leaders conducted two rounds of talks with opposition representatives. The precise outcome of these talks was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Bilawal announced local developments including a 35-million-gallon water treatment facility for Karachi's SITE area—projected to expand to 100 million gallons capacity—alongside pipeline projects for Lyari and DHA expected by October 20.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the joint discussions represent an effort by the government and PPP to ease political friction ahead of opposition demonstrations while maintaining national security. Bilawal stressed that counter-terrorism efforts must be treated as a unified national priority rather than a political issue. Additionally, the planned water initiatives aim to address regional deficits linked to the incomplete implementation of the 1991 Water Apportionment Accord.
