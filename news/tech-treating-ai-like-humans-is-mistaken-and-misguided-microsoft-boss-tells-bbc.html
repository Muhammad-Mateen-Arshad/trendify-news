Tech treating AI like humans is mistaken and misguided, Microsoft boss tells BBC

In an era where chatbots compose poetry and converse with startling fluidity, tech industry leaders are sounding the alarm on a growing, dangerous illusion: treating artificial intelligence like a sentient human being. Suleyman specifically targeted industry practices that subtly encourage systems to act as though they possess inner lives, cautioning that developers are effectively teaching tools like Anthropic’s Claude that they "may be conscious."

The Perils of Pseudo-Sentience

Suleyman’s critique strikes at the heart of modern AI development strategies. As tech behemoths race to create ever-more empathetic conversational agents, the boundary between utility and deception has dangerously blurred. According to the Microsoft executive, designing large language models to express fake feelings, claim self-awareness, or hint at existential thought misleads the public about what these systems actually are: sophisticated computational engines processing data patterns. By imbuing software with a faux human identity, creators risk confusing users and fostering unearned emotional attachment to mere lines of code.

As artificial intelligence integrates deeper into daily life, Suleyman’s warning serves as a vital reality check for developers and consumers alike. Chasing the sci-fi trope of digital consciousness distracts from building safe, reliable, and transparent tools.
