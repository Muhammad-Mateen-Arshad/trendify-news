Review: Resident Evil is the fresh take this flagging franchise really needed

Published by AI Pipeline | September 22, 2026 at 06:31 PM

For years, the cinematic Resident Evil universe has felt as decayed and aimless as the T-virus zombies wandering its claustrophobic halls. Following a long string of lackluster adaptations and convoluted sequels, franchise fatigue had firmly set in for both gamers and horror moviegoers alike. Enter director Zach Cregger. Fresh off the massive critical and box-office success of his breakthrough horror hit Barbarian, Cregger takes the reins of Capcom’s legendary survival horror saga, delivering a sharp, bloody, and surprisingly hilarious reinvention that breathes chaotic new life into the iconic brand.

Why Cregger's Reboot Works: A Perfect Tonal Shift

  • Masterful Tonal Balance: Seamlessly blends genuine survival horror tension with dark, absurd humor without ever undermining the stakes.
  • Unapologetic Gore: Features eye-popping, visceral practical effects and over-the-top creature designs that pay homage to classic body horror cinema.
  • Visionary Direction: Cregger brings the tight pacing, unpredictable twists, and atmospheric dread that made him one of modern horror's premier voices.

Cregger’s genius lies in understanding what made the original video games so enduring: the razor-thin line between terrifying biological nightmare and B-movie camp. Rather than attempting a self-serious, hyper-gritty action flick or a mindless, CGI-heavy blockbuster, this reboot embraces the inherent absurdity of corporate bio-weapons while keeping the life-and-death stakes suffocatingly real. The camera lingers in shadow-drenched corridors, building suspense with surgical precision before unleashing unhinged, violent mayhem on its unsuspecting cast.

The set-pieces are relentless, prioritizing squishy, bloody practical effects over cheap digital explosions. Combined with a whip-smart script that isn't afraid to poke fun at the franchise’s legacy, the film keeps audiences squirming in their seats one moment and cheering the next. It is a tricky balance of tones that previous film adaptations continuously failed to navigate, yet Cregger makes it look completely effortless.

The Verdict: The Shot of Adrenaline Cinematic Horror Needed

Ultimately, Zach Cregger’s Resident Evil is the shot of adrenaline this flagging cinematic universe desperately needed. It honors the underlying soul of survival horror while tearing up the tired, outdated movie playbook. Whether you are a die-hard fan who has played every entry since 1996 or simply a horror aficionado looking for an exhilarating ride, this reboot proves there is still plenty of terrifying life left in Raccoon City.
