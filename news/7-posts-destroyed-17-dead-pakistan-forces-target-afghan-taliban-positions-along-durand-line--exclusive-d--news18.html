Deepening Friction Over the Durand Line

The Durand Line, a 2,640-kilometer border drawn during the British colonial era, remains one of the world's most contested international boundaries. While Islamabad recognizes the line as its official western border, successive Afghan administrations—including the ruling Taliban regime—have adamantly rejected its legitimacy. This historical dispute has been further exacerbated in recent months by frequent skirmishes and allegations from Pakistan that the Taliban regime is harboring anti-Pakistan militant groups, such as the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan (TTP).
