Don't call it an SUV: The Ferrari Purosangue review

Published by AI Pipeline | September 20, 2026 at 03:31 PM

If you mention the "S-word" anywhere near Maranello, Ferrari executives will politely but firmly correct you. The legendary Italian automaker insists that its latest creation, the Ferrari Purosangue, is not a Sport Utility Vehicle. Instead, it is a high-riding, four-door sports car designed to deliver an unprecedented driving experience for four full-sized adults.

Redefining the Four-Seater Experience

Ferrari is no stranger to practical performance, having previously built four-seater grand tourers like the FF and the GTC4Lusso. However, those earlier models often suffered from awkward compromises—cramped rear quarters best reserved for short trips or extra luggage, wrapped in shooting-brake body styles. The Purosangue flips this historical script completely, proving far better tailored for human comfort than carrying bulky cargo.

Access to the rear cabin is granted via slick, electrically operated rear-hinged "welcome" doors. Inside, passengers are greeted not by a standard bench seat, but by two individual, fully adjustable bucket seats that mirror the support of the front cockpit. While traditional luxury crossovers brag about massive trunk space, the Purosangue offers a comparatively modest cargo area. Ferrari clearly built this machine to haul humans at breathtaking speeds, not gear for a weekend home renovation project.

Unapologetic V12 Supercar DNA

Despite its elevated posture, the heart of the Purosangue remains pure supercar. Maranello refused to compromise on mechanical grandeur, equipping the vehicle with a powertrain that stands alone in a market saturated with turbocharged luxury barges:

  • Naturally Aspirated 6.5-Liter V12: Screams all the way to an 8,250 RPM redline, pumping out a staggering 715 horsepower without relying on turbochargers or heavy hybrid batteries.
  • Revolutionary Active Suspension: Uses true electric-motor-driven dampers to eliminate body roll, allowing the high-riding chassis to corner as flatly as a traditional low-slung coupe.
  • Front-Mid Engine Architecture: Transaxle layout places the engine behind the front axle line, achieving an ideal 49:51 weight distribution for razor-sharp handling.

The Final Verdict

By prioritizing passenger ergonomics and dynamic agility over outright utility, Ferrari has successfully invented a brand-new category. The Purosangue offers the spine-tingling exhaust note, raw emotion, and sheer speed of a classic V12 flagship, but lets you share that thrill with three friends in complete luxury. Just remember the golden rule when discussing Maranello's newest masterwork: call it whatever you like, but do not call it an SUV.
