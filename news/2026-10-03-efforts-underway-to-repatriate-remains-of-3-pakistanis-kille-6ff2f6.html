Efforts underway to repatriate remains of 3 Pakistanis killed while recovering hijacked tanker from Somali pirates: PMO

Published by AI Pipeline | October 03, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, government arrangements are underway to repatriate the remains of three Pakistani sailors killed during a maritime operation off the coast of Somalia. The sailors died during an action by the Puntland Maritime Police Force to recover a hijacked oil tanker, the MT Honour 25. The remains of the deceased—identified as Rafiullah Khan, Syed Kashif Umer, and Mahmood Ahmed Ansari—were taken to Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, where Ethiopian Airlines is reviewing transfer procedures for a flight to Karachi following final approval.

Key facts

According to reports by Dawn, the incident involves several key details regarding the hijacking, casualties, and rescue effort:

  • The Palau-flagged MT Honour 25 was hijacked by Somali pirates on April 21 about 30 nautical miles off the Puntland region, carrying 19 crew members, including 10 Pakistanis, seven Indonesians, one Indian, and one Sri Lankan.
  • Somali maritime police conducted an operation on September 29 in the Dharin-Bar area of the Bandar Beyla district, taking control of the ship and arresting 21 suspected pirates.
  • Prior to the rescue, pirates killed five crew members, including three Pakistanis, one person from Myanmar, and one from India. Three other Pakistani crew members were injured in the operation.
  • Four Pakistani crew members—Yasir Khan, Aqeel Khan, Amin bin Shams, and Usman Ghani—were safely retrieved through efforts by Pakistan's Foreign Office and its embassy in Djibouti.
  • Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif announced a Rs12.5 million financial relief package for the families of the deceased, alongside promises of free housing and free education for their children.

Why it matters

As reported by Dawn, the rescue resolves a nearly five-month standoff following the vessel's capture. During the prolonged captivity, negotiations had stalled over a ransom demand of approximately $3 million, leaving the crew without sufficient food, clean water, or medicine. The Pakistani government has committed continuous assistance to support the families of the deceased and ensure the safe return of all surviving crew members.
