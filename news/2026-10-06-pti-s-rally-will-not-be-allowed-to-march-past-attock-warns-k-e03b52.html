PTI's rally will not be allowed to march past Attock, warns Kundi

Published on October 06, 2026

According to Dawn, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP) Governor Faisal Karim Kundi has declared that Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf's (PTI) long march will be stopped at Attock in Punjab and will not be allowed to proceed further. Speaking on Geo News, Kundi stated that the opposition lacks the capability to reach Islamabad. The rally was launched to demand the release of incarcerated PTI founder Imran Khan following the collapse of talks between the opposition and the federal government.

What happened

The protest march, headed by KP Chief Minister Sohail Afridi, moved forward despite federal warnings that political gatherings in the capital would be prohibited. Discussions between government officials and an opposition delegation broke down over disagreement regarding counterterrorism policies and visitation rights for Imran Khan. In response to the march, Governor Kundi raised concerns over security screening for participants and criticized the KP leadership's stance on regional militancy. He added that enforcing governor's rule in KP remains an option of last resort if directed by the president and prime minister.

Key facts

  • Route and schedule: The convoy started in Lakki Marwat and travelled through Karak and Kohat toward Peshawar, intending to reach Khairabad by October 8, where Chief Minister Afridi plans to reveal the next phase. Specific details regarding the final leg into the capital were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.
  • Negotiation deadlock: Weekend talks between government representatives and opposition figures ended without an agreement, primarily due to disputes over counterterrorism strategy and conditions surrounding Imran Khan's medical treatment and access.
  • Detentions in Sindh: PTI reported that law enforcement deployed personnel and detained more than 100 party leaders and workers across Sindh, including over 75 in Karachi.
  • Road restrictions: Movement across several districts in KP was restricted under Section 144, prompting former KP Chief Minister Ali Amin Gandapur to temporarily halt his group's advance from Yarik.

Why it matters

Dawn reported that the standoff underscores deepening political tensions between the federal authorities and the PTI-governed Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province. Beyond the conflict over Imran Khan's imprisonment, the disagreement reflects broader division over provincial security, military involvement, and counterterrorism approaches in the region.
