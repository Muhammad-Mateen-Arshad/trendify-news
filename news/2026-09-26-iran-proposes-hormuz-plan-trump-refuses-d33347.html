Iran proposes Hormuz plan, Trump refuses

Published by AI Pipeline | September 26, 2026

Iran has submitted a proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but United States President Donald Trump has turned down the offer, according to a report by Dawn. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi outlined the seven-day plan during United Nations meetings in New York, where he met with US envoy Steve Witkoff. The proposal was delivered to Washington through Qatari diplomatic channels.

What happened

According to Dawn, Tehran offered to restore normal maritime passage through the strategic waterway within seven days if certain terms were fulfilled. However, President Trump stated at the White House that he rejected the deal. Trump asserted that Iran put forward the proposal because it is experiencing heavy financial strain caused by a US military blockade. The full list of requirements demanded by the US was not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Key facts

  • Iranian terms: Reports indicate the proposal requested a seven-day pause in regional hostilities—including in Lebanon and Yemen—along with the release of at least $12 billion in frozen assets, an end to the US naval blockade, and the lifting of oil sanctions.
  • Previous breakdown: A June memorandum of understanding brokered by Pakistan collapsed after Iranian forces fired on commercial vessels accused of straying from approved shipping routes.
  • Official responses: Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian indicated readiness to abide by earlier terms if the US agreed, whereas Trump shared an image online designating the shipping channel as the "Trump Strait."

Why it matters

The Strait of Hormuz is essential to global oil supplies and remains the focal point of a nearly seven-month standoff between the United States and Iran, according to Dawn. Although recent UN meetings offered brief hope for diplomatic momentum, the rejection leaves the crisis unresolved, with reports suggesting a renewed aerial bombing campaign remains possible.
