Suicide blast targeting DI Khan checkpost claims 12 lives, injures 30

Published by AI Pipeline | September 26, 2026

What happened

According to Dawn, a suicide bombing at the Aman Mela police checkpost in Dera Ismail Khan’s Darazinda tehsil resulted in twelve deaths and 35 injuries. An unidentified attacker drove a tractor trolley laden with explosives into the post, destroying much of the facility and completely demolishing a neighboring house. Heavy gunfire was reported immediately following the blast.

In a separate attack shortly after the explosion, unidentified assailants fired upon a Rescue 1122 ambulance transporting victims' bodies from the scene. The shooting killed two rescue workers and injured a third, though emergency personnel persisted with transport and recovery efforts despite the security threat and local mobile network outages.

Key facts

  • The blast killed nine men, two women, a minor, and a Customs official; police confirmed no security personnel died in the explosion.
  • Twenty-five of the 35 injured individuals were taken to the district headquarters hospital for care.
  • In the ambulance attack, rescue workers Sharifullah and Imtiaz were killed, while worker Anees sustained serious injuries.
  • State media attributed the checkpost attack to Fitna al Khawarij, a government term for the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan, prompting an ongoing security operation.
  • More than 25 emergency personnel, ten ambulances, and a fire vehicle participated in completing the rescue and recovery operations.

Why it matters

According to Dawn, the violence drew swift condemnation across provincial and national leadership. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister Sohail Afridi urged the federal government to implement a policy shift on counterterrorism. Governor Faisal Karim Kundi called for unity and asked political leadership to postpone a protest march planned for October 4. Additionally, President Asif Ali Zardari, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi expressed condolences and vowed to intensify measures against anti-state militant networks.
