Cellular recycling changes with age, but not how we expected

Published by AI Pipeline | October 01, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests cellular recycling, known as autophagy, does not simply decline with age, but varies depending on biological sex and cell type.
  • Among participants aged 70 and older, higher cellular recycling rates were linked with lower physical performance metrics.
  • A small pilot study showed that mild exercise helped adjust blood cell recycling levels closer to those seen in younger adults while improving physical performance.

What the Source Says

Autophagy is the cellular process of breaking down damaged proteins and waste into reusable biological materials to protect against damage. In a study published in Aging Cell, scientists at the Sanford Burnham Prebys Medical Discovery Institute examined blood, skin, and engineered neuron cells from healthy volunteers aged 23 to 72 to evaluate how cellular recycling changes over time.

The investigators discovered that cellular recycling changes are not uniform across the body. In male skin cells, recycling rates dropped with age, whereas they stayed stable in female skin cells and rose in female neurons. Furthermore, direct measurements of recycling activity differed from what static gene expression levels predicted.

In adults aged 70 and older, higher cellular recycling was surprisingly connected to lower aerobic fitness, reduced grip strength, lower metabolism, higher resting heart rate, and increased body mass index across different cell types. However, when five participants aged 77 to 88 completed a 12-week mild exercise program focused on fall prevention, their blood cell recycling shifted toward more youthful levels alongside improved physical function.

Practical Notes

Research suggests that physical activity may influence cellular processes, but specific exercise prescriptions for the general public are not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Always talk to your doctor before beginning any new exercise regimen or making changes to your physical routine.
