Telehealth companies keep exposing their customers' medical data. What should they do?

Published by AI Pipeline | September 21, 2026

Connecting with your doctor from the comfort of your living room is one of the greatest modern conveniences. It is no surprise that telehealth services have skyrocketed in popularity, especially for adults enjoying their golden years. Skipping long drives, crowded waiting rooms, and bad weather has made managing personal wellness easier and more comfortable than ever.

However, recent news highlights a growing concern that deserves our attention: several popular telehealth companies have repeatedly exposed their customers' private medical data. While virtual care is here to stay, taking a moment to understand your privacy rights can help you navigate online healthcare safely.

Why This Matters for Seniors

Your personal health information is deeply private. From prescription histories to specific diagnoses, this data belongs solely to you and your trusted medical team. Unfortunately, recent investigations revealed that some online health platforms shared patient tracking data with social media advertisers or failed to use proper encryption to prevent security breaches.

For seniors, this issue goes beyond a simple breach of trust. Exposed medical details can leave you vulnerable to aggressive targeted spam, misleading health ads, or even sophisticated identity scams. While technology offers incredible freedom, protecting your digital footprint is an essential part of staying healthy and secure.

Simple Steps to Protect Your Privacy

You do not need to give up the convenience of virtual appointments. Instead, adopting a few simple habits can help ensure your health records remain strictly confidential:

  • Choose established providers: Whenever possible, use telehealth portals hosted directly by your long-time primary physician, local hospital network, or Medicare plan rather than third-party app start-ups.
  • Review the privacy policy: Look for a quick statement on the company's website promising that they do not sell or share your medical data for marketing purposes.
  • Secure your patient portal: Ensure your online accounts use strong, unique passwords, and avoid logging in while connected to public Wi-Fi networks.
  • Ask direct questions: Do not hesitate to call your doctor’s office and ask what specific security measures they use to safeguard your virtual visits.

Telehealth remains a wonderful, empowering tool that allows you to take control of your well-being on your own terms. By staying informed and taking a few basic precautions, you can continue to enjoy safe, high-quality healthcare right from home.
