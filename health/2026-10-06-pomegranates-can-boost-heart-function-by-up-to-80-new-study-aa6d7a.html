Pomegranates Can Boost Heart Function by Up to 80%, New Study Finds

Published on October 06, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests a natural compound produced during pomegranate digestion can aid heart muscle relaxation.
  • Antioxidants in pomegranates help shield both LDL and HDL cholesterol from oxidative damage.
  • Studies indicate pomegranate consumption may help manage blood pressure and slow down the narrowing of arteries.

What the Source Says

According to research published in the journal Science Advances, digesting pomegranates causes the body to generate a chemical called urolithin A. Laboratory findings indicate that this compound acts like a molecular switch, activating a protein that assists heart muscle relaxation. In animal models suffering from a stiff-heart condition, treatment with urolithin A enhanced heart function by up to 80% compared to untreated models. The compound also decreased harmful tissue scarring, stopped dangerous cell enlargement, and demonstrated similar relaxing benefits on lab-engineered human heart tissue.

In addition, a 2013 study published in the Rambam Maimonides Medical Journal highlights that pomegranates contain strong antioxidants, including tannins and anthocyanins. Research suggests these antioxidants defend LDL cholesterol from oxidation and help HDL cholesterol perform better. Because the antioxidants bind to natural fruit sugars, they are reported to be safe for diabetic patients. The source notes that pomegranate juice helped improve artery elasticity, lower blood pressure, and slow plaque accumulation in individuals at moderate risk for coronary heart disease.

Practical Notes

Although urolithin A is considered safe for human consumption, researchers state that human clinical trials are necessary before developing standard medical treatments. Specific dietary amounts and serving sizes are not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Always talk to your doctor before making changes to your diet or health routine.
