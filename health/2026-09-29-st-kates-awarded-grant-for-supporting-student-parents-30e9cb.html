St. Kate’s awarded grant for supporting student parents

Published by AI Pipeline | September 29, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • St. Catherine University received $112,200 from state agencies to assist students raising children.
  • The grant helps lower financial stress by covering expenses for child care, legal guidance in family law, and mental health co-pays.
  • New educational programming includes bi-weekly virtual wellness sessions focused on mind-body practices for families.
  • Ongoing university services include monthly family nutrition instruction and dedicated family-friendly study areas.

What the Source Says

According to updates from the Minnesota Office of Higher Education and the Minnesota Department of Health, St. Catherine University was awarded $112,200 through the Student Parent Support Initiative. The university first received this grant funding in 2024. Student parents account for an average of 19 percent of the student body across the university's three colleges. Full application requirements and individual grant distributions are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

The funding is managed by the university's Access and Success department, an office that has provided resources to student parents for over 30 years. Newly enrolled student parents receive starter kits detailing available services. These resources feature emergency assistance funds, a child-friendly study space in the library with an attached playroom, and once-a-month nutrition education sessions for students and their family members.

The recent grant introduces two main additions to the campus community:

  • Family Wellness Sessions: Bi-weekly virtual meetings that offer structured guidance for parents and children to practice mind-body wellness strategies together.
  • Student Parent Advisory Board: A board comprising one undergraduate and one graduate student to provide direct input regarding the needs of student parents.

The announcement coincided with Student Parent Month, marked by a September 22 roundtable discussion with university administration, student parents, and the state's higher education commissioner.

Practical Notes

Reports from the state organizations suggest that holistic family assistance—such as offering co-pay relief for mental health services and virtual mind-body exercises—can help lower daily stress and support overall well-being.
