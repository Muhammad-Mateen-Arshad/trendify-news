NIH makes major investments to advance human-based research infrastructure and technologies

Published by AI Pipeline | September 27, 2026

Key Takeaways

Research suggests that modern laboratory approaches may help scientists better understand disease by focusing directly on human biological systems. To support this effort, the National Institutes of Health (NIH) has announced five broad projects designed to update research tools and reduce reliance on animal testing.

  • More than $88 million is dedicated to ten construction projects across nine states to expand human-based testing infrastructure.
  • A specialized laboratory at the NIH Clinical Center will integrate standardized human organoids, robotics, and artificial intelligence.
  • Other programs include a multi-million dollar technology challenge, recruiting specialized grant reviewers, and evaluating ways to track animal use.

What the Source Says

According to the NIH, the agency is expanding investments in human-based technologies to make preclinical research faster and more predictive of human health. The strategy centers on five main actions intended to build scientific infrastructure nationwide.

First, over $88 million will fund ten construction projects in nine states, focusing on New Approach Methodologies (NAMs). These facilities aim to investigate health issues such as cancer, chronic disease, obesity, metabolic dysfunction, environmental health, and rare childhood diseases. Funding will also support regions that historically lag in healthcare research capacity.

Second, the NIH launched the Qu-SAFE Challenge, offering over $7 million in potential prize awards. This initiative encourages developers to use quantum technology and tools like organ-on-a-chip devices to measure biological signals and evaluate drug safety. Third, the NIH plans to build the Bio Genesis Autonomous Human Biology Laboratory. This facility will combine standardized human organoid models, robotics, data systems, and artificial intelligence to run reproducible experiments.

Fourth, the NIH is actively seeking grant reviewers with expertise in human-based science to evaluate incoming research proposals. Fifth, the agency released a request for information to study the feasibility and potential impacts of tracking and reporting the annual number of live vertebrate animals used in NIH-funded research.

Practical Notes

While animal models remain a part of biomedical research, the NIH aims to lower their use whenever feasible. Specific submission deadlines, exact project timelines, and individual institutional recipients were not specified in the source; please check the official notice for those details.
