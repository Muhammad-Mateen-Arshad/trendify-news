The Best Late-Night Drink to Support Healthy Aging, According to Dietitians

Published by AI Pipeline | October 03, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Milk provides essential protein and calcium to support muscle maintenance and bone strength in older adults.
  • Research suggests dairy consumption may contribute to improved sleep quality, longer sleep duration, and better cognitive health.
  • Dietitians suggest opting for 1% or 2% milk, as higher fat levels in dairy may accelerate cellular aging.
  • A overall healthy lifestyle also relies on a balanced diet, physical activity, social ties, and consistent medical care.

What the Source Says

According to information reviewed by Dietitian Jane Leverich, M.S., RDN, milk serves as a nutrient-dense option that offers significant nutritional value for aging adults. Dietitian Danielle VenHuizen, M.S., RD, explains that milk contains highly digestible proteins. When paired with resistance training, research suggests that the whey protein found in dairy can help boost muscle growth in older individuals.

Alongside protein, calcium plays a key role in maintaining bodily function. Dietitian Amy Brownstein, M.S., RD, notes that calcium supports bone and muscle health, which helps lower the risk of fractures that might otherwise lead to physical decline or a loss of independence. One cup of milk supplies roughly 309 milligrams of calcium, representing about 30% of the Daily Value.

Regarding cellular health, research suggests that the fat content in milk may affect telomere length, which is linked to cellular aging. Higher fat intake from milk is associated with faster cellular aging, leading dietitians to recommend 1% or 2% milk options. Additionally, milk contains calcium and magnesium, which research suggests may assist the body in relaxing and improving overall sleep quality and duration. Dairy foods are also linked by research to potential brain health benefits, which can be enjoyed alongside snacks like berries, nuts, or whole-grain crackers.

Practical Notes

Healthy aging depends on a broader combination of habits alongside proper nutrition:

  • Diet: Focus on eating plans rich in protein, fiber, and antioxidants to support longevity.
  • Movement: Participate in regular exercise and strength training to support muscle health, blood flow, and cognitive quality of life.
  • Socialization: Keep up with regular social activities, as research suggests a lack of social connection is associated with physical frailty.
  • Healthcare: Maintain routine appointments with medical professionals to identify and address health concerns early.

Please consult your doctor for personalized advice before making substantial changes to your health or diet routines.
