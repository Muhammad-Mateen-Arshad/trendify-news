Alzheimer’s risk linked to compound found in popular joint health supplements

Published on October 09, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests a possible connection between glucosamine supplements and accelerated cognitive decline in individuals with existing cognitive impairment.
  • In an observational study, patients with mild cognitive impairment taking glucosamine were 25% more likely to progress to dementia compared to non-users.
  • The research did not study cognitively healthy adults and does not suggest that glucosamine poses a risk to individuals taking it solely for joint health.

What the Source Says

A study led by University of Florida researchers and published in Nature Metabolism investigated the relationship between glucosamine—a compound commonly taken to support aging joints—and dementia risk. To understand its effects, researchers analyzed patient medical records, tracked laboratory mice, and examined human brain tissue.

The investigation centered on glycosylation, a biological process where brain cells attach sugar chains to proteins to help them fold and communicate correctly. Glucosamine acts as a building block for these sugar chains and bypasses the body's normal regulatory controls. Researchers observed an excess of sugar chains on proteins in Alzheimer's-affected human brains, which research suggests may disrupt how nerve cells interact.

When analyzing medical records, researchers found that patients with mild cognitive impairment who took glucosamine had a 25% higher likelihood of progressing to dementia, though their mortality risk remained unchanged. Among patients who already had dementia, glucosamine users experienced higher mortality than non-users. In animal trials, healthy mice given glucosamine showed no memory changes, but mice with an Alzheimer's-like condition developed higher sugar chain levels and performed worse on memory tests.

Practical Notes

Because the study relied on existing health records, it cannot prove that glucosamine causes cognitive decline or Alzheimer's disease. Specific details regarding supplement brand, dosage, and duration of use were Not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Researchers note that these findings serve as a safety signal pointing to the need for controlled clinical trials rather than a definitive warning against joint supplements for everyone. Always talk to your doctor before starting or stopping any dietary supplement.
