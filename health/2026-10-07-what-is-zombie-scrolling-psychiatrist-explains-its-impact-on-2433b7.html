What Is Zombie Scrolling? Psychiatrist Explains Its Impact on Mental Health

Published on October 07, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Zombie scrolling describes the habit of endlessly viewing online content without a clear purpose or lasting memory of what was consumed.
  • Research suggests that continuous screen overstimulation can weaken attention spans, executive planning skills, and short-term memory.
  • Establishing phone-free routines and choosing offline activities can help restore focus, sleep, and overall daily balance.

What the Source Says

Dr Sharadhi C, Consultant Psychiatry at Aster CMI Hospital in Bangalore, explains that zombie scrolling is an automatic habit where individuals continuously browse social media feeds, short videos, or news updates. This behavior frequently happens while eating, traveling, watching television, or lying in bed. Dr Sharadhi notes that spending excessive time on mindless browsing reduces opportunities for genuine rest, social interaction, and physical activity. It can also increase feelings of frustration, anxiety, or information overload, particularly when scrolling late at night delays bedtime and keeps the mind active.

Additionally, a 2025 study published in the journal Brain Sciences suggests that excessive digital overstimulation can lead to noticeable drops in focus, creating shortened attention spans. The research indicates that constantly switching attention between brief digital posts can disrupt executive functions, which are the mental skills used to plan days, organize thoughts, and make logical decisions. The study also notes that heavy digital users regularly experience forgetfulness and mental fatigue, warning that chronic overstimulation might make individuals more vulnerable to premature cognitive decline or early onset dementia later in life.

Practical Notes

To help manage mindless browsing habits, Dr Sharadhi suggests taking intentional steps to regain control over screen time:

  • Pause before opening an app to clarify your exact intention and decide how long you will stay.
  • Disable non-essential notifications, set built-in screen-time limits, or remove distracting apps from the main screen.
  • Establish phone-free periods during meals, social conversations, and the hour right before bedtime.
  • Replace screen time with restorative offline activities, such as walking, reading, exercising, listening to music, or spending time with others.
  • Address underlying stress or seek professional support if digital browsing severely affects your sleep, mood, or daily relationships.
