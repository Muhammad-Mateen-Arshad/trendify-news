Are You Making These 5 Condom Mistakes? An Expert Weighs In on Safe Sex

Published on October 05, 2026

Condoms serve as physical barrier devices during sexual contact to reduce the risk of unintended pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Research suggests that their success relies heavily on correct and consistent use rather than manufacturing quality.

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests that male condoms offer over 90 percent protection against major STIs like HIV, Hepatitis B, and gonorrhoea when used properly every time.
  • Primary condom failures stem from human error during application and use rather than factory defects.
  • Barrier methods provide physical protection against disease transmission that alternative methods, such as birth control pills or permanent sterilisation, cannot offer.

What the Source Says

Dr. Lavanya Kiran, Director and Lead Consultant of Obstetrics and Gynaecology at KIMS Hospitals in Electronic City, Bengaluru, outlines five common errors that compromise condom effectiveness:

  • Applying the condom too late: It must be put on before any genital contact, as pre-ejaculate may contain sperm and infectious fluids.
  • Using damaged products: Expired condoms or compromised packaging can result in tears during use.
  • Choosing incorrect lubricants: Substances like oils, petroleum jelly, and creams weaken latex. Water-based or silicone lubricants are suitable choices.
  • Layering two condoms: Using two condoms simultaneously increases friction and raises the chance of tearing.
  • Reusing condoms: They are designed for single use only. A fresh condom is required for each act of intercourse, and holding the base during withdrawal prevents slippage and spillage.

Additionally, a 2015 study published in the Indian Journal of Sexually Transmitted Diseases and AIDS highlights that female condoms are the only barrier method entirely initiated and controlled by women. The study notes that while STI vaccines remain in clinical trials, physical barriers remain vital for public health, and manufacturers are working on modern materials like graphene to enhance user experience.

Practical Notes

Always inspect condom packaging for damage and check expiration dates prior to use. If a condom breaks or slips during intercourse, an emergency response plan may be required. Specific steps for emergency contraception are not specified in the source; please check the official notice. Always talk to your doctor for personal medical advice regarding sexual health and contraceptive choices.
