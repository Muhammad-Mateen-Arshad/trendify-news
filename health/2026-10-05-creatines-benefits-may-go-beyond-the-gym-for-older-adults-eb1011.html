Creatine’s Benefits May Go Beyond the Gym for Older Adults

Published on October 05, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests creatine monohydrate may support aging adults in gaining lean tissue and strength, both with and without formal workout routines.
  • When combined with regular exercise and a calorie-reduced diet, creatine helped study participants reduce body fat while maintaining muscle mass.
  • A review of clinical trials noted no significant difference in side effect rates between creatine and a placebo.

What the Source Says

A 12-week clinical study published in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition evaluated 64 healthy adults aged 45 to 65. Led by Richard Kreider, professor and director of the Exercise and Sport Nutrition Lab at Texas A&M University, the trial examined the effects of taking 10 grams of creatine monohydrate daily compared to a placebo. The research was funded by the WoodNext Foundation via the Texas A&M Foundation.

Participants chose whether to join a structured diet and workout program or remain inactive. Among individuals in the non-exercise group, research suggests those taking creatine gained roughly 2.4 pounds of lean tissue and improved their leg and bench press strength. For participants enrolled in the exercise program—who performed aerobic and resistance training three days a week while maintaining a modest calorie deficit—adding creatine led to greater improvements in bench press strength, leg press strength, and time to exhaustion. These active participants gained nearly three pounds of lean tissue and experienced a greater drop in body fat percentage compared to the placebo group. Creatine did not, however, show an added benefit for aerobic capacity.

Practical Notes

Creatine is a naturally occurring compound that helps replenish energy inside cells. While it is present in foods like fish and meat, obtaining 10 grams daily through whole foods would require eating five to 10 pounds of meat or fish each day. For this reason, supplementation is highlighted as a more practical option.

In a 2025 review of 685 clinical trials, researchers found that side effect rates for creatine were not significantly different from a placebo. Specific brand recommendations or details on potential drug interactions were not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Please consult your doctor before adding any new supplement to your routine or making changes to your dietary and physical activity habits.
