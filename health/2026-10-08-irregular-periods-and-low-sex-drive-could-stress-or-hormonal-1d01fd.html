Irregular Periods and Low Sex Drive: Could Stress or Hormonal Imbalance Be the Reason?

Published on October 08, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Irregular menstrual cycles and decreased sexual desire can share underlying causes, including hormone shifts, health conditions, and prolonged stress.
  • Research suggests that hormones such as oestrogen, thyroid hormones, and prolactin influence both reproductive function and sexual wellbeing.
  • Medications, sleep quality, and stress levels can independently alter libido and menstrual timing.

What the Source Says

Dr Sunita Goyal, Senior Consultant in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology at Cloudnine Group of Hospitals in Ludhiana, explains that occasional cycle variations or temporary changes in sexual desire do not automatically indicate a medical issue. However, when these changes occur together or persist for months, medical evaluation is appropriate.

According to a study published in The Journal of Sex Research, reproductive function and sexual desire depend on complex hormonal interactions. Oestrogen influences the menstrual cycle, vaginal lubrication, and sexual wellbeing. Other hormones, including progesterone, testosterone, thyroid hormones, and prolactin, also affect these systems. Specific conditions associated with cycle or libido changes include PMOS (previously known as PCOS), thyroid disorders, elevated prolactin, perimenopause, and low energy availability caused by severe weight loss, restrictive eating, or extreme exercise.

A review in Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews notes that the brain manages the hormones responsible for ovulation. Ongoing stress can disrupt this brain signaling, which may delay or prevent ovulation. Stress, poor sleep, and exhaustion can also decrease libido independently of hormonal conditions. Additionally, certain medications, including some antidepressants and hormonal contraceptives, may alter sexual desire.

Practical Notes

Maintaining a record of menstrual cycles, stress, sleep, physical activity, weight, and medications can help a healthcare provider identify relevant patterns. Individuals should not stop taking prescribed medications without medical guidance, even if they suspect a side effect.

Professional guidance is recommended if cycle irregularities persist or coincide with symptoms like severe pelvic pain, unexplained weight changes, persistent fatigue, acne, or unusual breast discharge. You should talk to your doctor to determine the underlying cause of any ongoing health changes.
