Fossil Discovery: Rare mastodon jaw unearthed in Orange County, New York

Published on October 06, 2026

While doing yard chores at his property near Scotchtown in Orange County, New York, a homeowner noticed unusual, rounded objects emerging through some plants. Initially mistaking the items for baseballs, he picked them up and realized they were massive fossilized teeth. After clearing a few inches of soil, he uncovered two additional teeth and contacted paleontology experts to examine the site.

The Discovery

Teams from the New York State Museum and the State University of New York Orange carried out an excavation at the residential property. The dig recovered an intact mastodon jaw belonging to an adult animal. According to state officials, this discovery marks the first complete mastodon jaw unearthed in New York state in more than 11 years.

What Scientists Found

In addition to the adult jaw, researchers retrieved other skeletal remnants from the property, including a rib fragment and a piece of toe bone. A museum scientist noted that while the complete jaw is the primary attraction of the find, the surrounding bones provide valuable context and potential for additional research. The specimens were slated for scientific analysis, with plans for the museum to display them to the public in 2025.

Why It Matters

The anonymous homeowner expressed excitement that his property yielded an important scientific contribution. The event highlights Orange County's significance in prehistoric research:

  • More than 150 mastodon fossils have been discovered across New York state, with roughly one-third of them located in Orange County.
  • Mastodons were distant relatives of modern elephants that roamed North America between 3.75 million and 11,000 years ago.
  • These prehistoric mammals stood 8 to 10 feet tall and weighed up to six tons.
