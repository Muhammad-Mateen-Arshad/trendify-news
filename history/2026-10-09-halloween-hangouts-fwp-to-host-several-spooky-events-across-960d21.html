Halloween Hangouts: FWP to Host Several Spooky Events Across the State

Published on October 09, 2026

The Discovery

Montana Fish, Wildlife & Parks is hosting a variety of seasonal events during October, offering visitors opportunities to learn about prehistoric life and local history. At Makoshika State Park, an annual event dedicated to National Fossil Day allows attendees to act as paleontologists. Guests can learn about the formation of fossils, the primary types that exist, and the specific kinds of fossils preserved within the park itself.

What Scientists Found

Details regarding specific scientific discoveries, fossil measurements, or exact species names are not specified in the source; please check the official notice. However, the available programs focus on educational dinosaur activities alongside animal anatomy. For instance, the Skull Detective session at Montana WILD gives participants an up-close look at real animal skulls, using physical clues to explain how various species search for food, evade danger, and navigate their environments.

Why It Matters

These events connect public outdoor recreation with paleontology and regional historical study. Beyond fossil science, attendees can explore several other historical topics across the state. Highlights include:

  • Guided nighttime walks at Pictograph Cave State Park focused on the area's Native American history.
  • Educational sessions at Travelers' Rest State Park centered on the historical Lewis and Clark Corps of Discovery.
  • Ghost walks at Bannack State Park presenting scenes derived from actual historic incidents.
  • Storytelling at Makoshika State Park covering mysterious Montana folklore and indigenous legends.
