Twist in Noah’s Ark mystery as ‘ancient city’ discovered underwater near final resting place

Published by AI Pipeline | October 04, 2026

The Discovery

Explorers diving into the depths of Lake Van, the largest lake in Turkey covering approximately 1,450 square miles, have documented the remains of an ancient submerged settlement. The site was initially encountered by underwater filmmaker Tahsin Ceylan in 2017. However, recent video footage has revealed the true reach of the ruins, bringing long-hidden stone walls into clear view.

What Scientists Found

Located more than 75 feet beneath the surface of the water, divers uncovered several distinct architectural features crafted by an ancient culture:

  • A shaped circular stone structure bordered by three steps.
  • A stonework wall standing eight feet tall along with interlocking brickwork.
  • A causeway extending 150 feet outward from the central stonework.

Researcher Matthew LaCroix, a member of the dive team, explained that what has been documented so far may only be a small portion of a much larger building complex. Further details regarding exact excavation schedules are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why It Matters

The age and history of the site remain topics of debate among experts. Evaluating geological data from nearby volcanic eruptions, LaCroix proposes that the ruins might be at least 11,700 years old, suggesting an advanced culture built temples and structures well before traditional timelines. Conversely, other archaeologists date the location to much later eras, suggesting it originates from the Urartian period around 3,000 years ago, or the medieval period about 1,550 years ago.

The location also draws attention because it sits approximately 90 miles away from the Durupınar formation on Mount Tendürek, a geological feature that some treat as the petrified resting place of Noah's Ark. Regardless of its exact era, the underwater masonry offers an intriguing new chapter in ancient history.
