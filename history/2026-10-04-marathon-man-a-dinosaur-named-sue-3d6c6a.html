Marathon Man: A Dinosaur named SUE

Published on October 04, 2026

The Discovery

In August 1990, paleontologist Sue Hendrickson uncovered an extraordinary fossil in South Dakota. Officially cataloged as FMNH PR 2081 and popularly known as SUE, this Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton lived roughly 67 million years ago. SUE is recognized as the most complete, best-preserved, and one of the largest T-Rex skeletons ever unearthed. Excavations successfully recovered 250 of an estimated 380 bones, representing about 90 percent of the skeleton by bulk.

What Scientists Found

Research on the physical specimen shows that SUE measured 40.5 feet (12.3 metres) long and reached 13 feet (4 metres) in height at the hips. Paleontologists estimate that SUE weighed between 8.4 and 14 metric tons during its lifetime. Bone studies indicate SUE reached 28 to 33 years of age, making this individual one of the oldest T-Rex specimens ever analyzed.

In addition to its size, the skeleton displays widespread pathologies revealing a violent and painful life. SUE suffered numerous health conditions and injuries, including:

  • Ribs that had been fractured and subsequently healed
  • A torn shoulder tendon
  • A severe bone infection known as osteomyelitis in the left leg
  • Arthritis within the tail vertebrae

Why It Matters

SUE offers an unprecedented glimpse into the tough realities faced by ancient apex predators. Although SUE reigned at the top of the food chain, the extensive physical damage indicates a difficult existence filled with severe trauma and illness, yet the dinosaur managed to survive into old age against the odds.

This impressive specimen has been shared with the public through displays like "SUE The T-Rex Experience" at the Telus Spark Science Centre in Calgary's Nose Creek Valley. Visitors to such dinosaur exhibits can also learn about fossil hunting through media such as a 40-minute film narrated by the late Sam Neill, which details the true story of three teenagers who discovered a T-Rex leg fossil in North Dakota's Hell Creek Badlands.
