Mr. Morioka, is it true that 'dinosaurs are immortal'?—Thinking about how to use dinosaurs as content at Junglia

Published by AI Pipeline | September 27, 2026

The Discovery

Our understanding of ancient life is constantly shifting as new fossils are uncovered and reexamined. Unlike fictional icons that stay static over time, prehistoric creatures are subject to ongoing reinterpretation. Recent scientific progress shows that traditional depictions of famous dinosaurs, from land-dwelling theropods to massive river predators, are far more fluid than modern pop culture often suggests.

What Scientists Found

Dinosaur paleontology has produced several major updates regarding the physical traits and habits of iconic species:

  • Large Theropods: A study published in Science in 2023 indicated that large theropods like Tyrannosaurus rex may have had their teeth covered by soft tissue when their mouths were closed, challenging the traditional look of exposed teeth.
  • Spinosaurus debate: In 2020, a paper in Nature reported a fossil tail that was vertically broad, suggesting it was used to propel the animal underwater. A 2022 study in Nature supported an underwater foraging lifestyle based on bone density analysis. However, a 2024 paper in PLOS ONE questioned the validity of that bone density method and disputed the idea that Spinosaurus actively dived to hunt prey underwater.

Why It Matters

These scientific shifts create a distinct challenge for permanent displays. For example, at a January 28, 2025 press conference, Tsuyoshi Morioka discussed using dinosaurs as non-proprietary content for the Junglia theme park in Okinawa. The park's DINOSAUR SAFARI attraction places 20 dinosaur installations across approximately 45,000 square meters, featuring a 19-meter Brachiosaurus and a Tyrannosaurus attack. Unlike copyrighted characters such as Mickey Mouse or Mario, whose appearances are controlled entirely by rights holders, physical dinosaur models are vulnerable to obsolescence. When new fossil discoveries alter what scientists know about dinosaur posture, skin, feathers, or mouth structure, fixed physical structures cannot easily be changed, turning scientific progress into a potential risk for long-term exhibits.
