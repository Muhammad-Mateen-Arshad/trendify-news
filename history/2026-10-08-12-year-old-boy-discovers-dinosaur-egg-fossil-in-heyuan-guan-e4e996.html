12-year-old boy discovers dinosaur egg fossil in Heyuan, Guangdong

Published on October 08, 2026

The Discovery

A 12-year-old boy named Chen Zixi uncovered an ancient fossil near his home in Shuangxia Village, located in Heyuan, Guangdong. Beneath a tree situated near a flower bed, he spotted a well-preserved, roughly spherical dinosaur egg fossil measuring approximately 14 centimeters in diameter. Following his discovery, Chen brought the specimen directly to the Heyuan Dinosaur Museum on October 3.

What Scientists Found

Using the information provided by the young boy, researchers visited the location to investigate. During their search, experts identified a second dinosaur egg fossil under a nearby tree. Local residents had previously placed this second specimen in the flower bed as a decorative piece. Both fossils were subsequently transferred to the museum for preservation.

Scientists plan to confirm the exact location where these fossils were originally unearthed and will conduct further studies on them. Specific details regarding the exact dinosaur species or prehistoric era are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

Why It Matters

This recent encounter echoes a key historical milestone from 30 years prior. On March 6, 1996, a group of children in the very same village located Heyuan's first documented dinosaur egg nest. That initial event launched the city's ongoing initiative to protect and analyze its fossil resources. Key facts regarding the city's paleontological history include:

  • Heyuan is officially known as the "Hometown of Dinosaurs in China."
  • The Heyuan Dinosaur Museum currently holds a collection exceeding 20,000 dinosaur egg fossils.
  • In 2004, the museum was awarded a Guinness World Record for its collection of 10,008 dinosaur egg fossils.
