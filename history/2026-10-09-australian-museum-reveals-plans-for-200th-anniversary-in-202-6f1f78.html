Australian Museum reveals plans for 200th Anniversary in 2027

Published on October 09, 2026

The Australian Museum, established in 1827 as the nation's first museum, has announced a year-long program called AM200 to mark its 200th anniversary in 2027. Holding over 22 million specimens and objects, it houses the largest collection in the Southern Hemisphere. The upcoming celebrations will showcase international exhibitions, scientific projects, First Nations cultural initiatives, and community events.

The Discovery

A major highlight of the anniversary centers on Earth's prehistoric past. Opening in late November 2026, the museum will host an exhibition titled "The World's Biggest Dinosaur: Dinosaurs of Patagonia." This feature will present a full cast of the largest complete dinosaur fossil ever uncovered, stretching across an international touring hall space exceeding 38 meters in length. Specific details regarding when the original fossil was excavated are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

What Scientists Found

In addition to ancient giant life, the institution will highlight broader planetary history through "Our Story with David Attenborough," opening in March 2027. This 50-minute, 360-degree cinematic experience surrounds visitors with imagery across four walls and the floor, tracing the history of Earth and humanity from its origins to the future. It marks the first time this project will be presented outside of Europe.

The museum is also launching several initiatives to share its scientific and historical work:

  • A Museum-wide Discovery Trail leading visitors through key objects, specimens, and historical findings.
  • An online platform featuring 200 of the institution's most significant discoveries since 1827.
  • A new one-hour documentary and an illustrated history book examining two centuries of scientific research.
  • The interactive "Museum Memories" project, inviting the public to submit personal stories online.

Why It Matters

The bicentennial provides an opportunity to reflect on historical responsibilities while emphasizing First Nations knowledge and modern environmental science. The museum will unveil a new permanent artwork in Hintze Hall titled Kalikina Country, created by Tasmanian Aboriginal Elder Aunty Lola Greeno and artist Vanessa Greeno. Additionally, an exhibition named Message Sticks will showcase traditional Indigenous communication methods, connection, and Lore. Through the Australian Museum Research Institute, the organization continues to advance research into climate change, wildlife conservation, and the environment across Australia and the Pacific.
