What happens when the State Department loses its institutional memory?

Published on October 07, 2026

The U.S. State Department currently faces a substantial number of open diplomatic positions worldwide. While ten new ambassadors were confirmed by the U.S. Senate prior to recess on September 30, 2026—covering posts in Kenya, Nicaragua, Malaysia, and Kyrgyzstan—a large portion of leadership roles across foreign missions remain open.

Role Overview

The primary positions involved are U.S. Ambassador and Deputy Chief of Mission (DCM). Out of 196 total ambassador posts, 92 positions are currently unfilled. Unstaffed ambassador posts include locations such as Belarus, Bulgaria, Egypt, Guatemala, Haiti, Indonesia, Kuwait, Mauritania, Pakistan, and Qatar. These roles serve as top diplomatic figureheads responsible for advancing security, economic, and strategic goals, as well as managing communication between host countries and Washington.

Eligibility Criteria

Requirements for diplomatic leadership positions mentioned in the source depend on the appointment track:

  • Career Diplomats: Positions are typically filled by senior foreign service officers who possess at least 20 years of diplomatic experience.
  • Political Appointees: Nominations put forward by the White House have included associates, friends, and campaign donors, sometimes without extensive government or overseas experience.
