Trump suspends Microsoft from green card programme amid H-1B crackdown

Published on October 08, 2026

Role Overview

The US administration has suspended Microsoft and several prominent technology firms from a federal programme that allows foreign workers to apply for green cards. Announced by Vice President JD Vance, the suspension also applies to Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata, Wipro, HCL, and Capgemini. The policy change marks an increase in government pressure regarding foreign worker and international student visa programmes, including H-1B, J-1, and exchange visas.

Key Details

Key facts and statistics regarding the visa updates include:

  • Green Card Advertising Rules: Under the suspended programme, companies must advertise open job roles in local American media to prove no local worker is available before requesting a green card through the Department of Labor.
  • Employer Statistics: Microsoft, located in Redmond, Washington, is the fifth-largest H-1B employer in the US, behind Amazon in first place. Last year, Microsoft acquired 6,300 H-1B visas and nearly 3,000 green cards while laying off 6,000 domestic workers.
  • Microsoft's Response: Microsoft stated that 80 percent of its 6,000 H-1B filings in the last financial year were status extensions or changes for existing staff, while filings for new employees accounted for just 1 percent of its US workforce.
  • Proposed Visa Fees: The administration has proposed a $103,265 fee for H-1B work visas. Additionally, a $70,000 fee has been proposed for universities for each international student participating in the Optional Practical Training (OPT) work programme.
  • Executive Actions: A recent executive order instructs federal agencies to implement measures preventing abuse of the H-1B visa programme to protect local workers.
