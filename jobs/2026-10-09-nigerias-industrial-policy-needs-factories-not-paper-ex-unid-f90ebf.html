Nigeria’s industrial policy needs factories, not paper – Ex-UNIDO chief

Published on October 09, 2026

Role Overview

This news briefing reviews major industry developments discussed by former United Nations Industrial Development Organisation director-general Kandeh Yumkella at the 6th Adeola Odutola Lecture of the Manufacturers Association of Nigeria in Lagos. Addressing Nigeria's industrial policy introduced on February 17, Yumkella stressed that genuine progress must be tracked by real manufacturing output, domestic value creation, and new factory establishments rather than theoretical plans.

The policy framework aims to transition the national economy away from raw commodity exports toward high-value manufacturing. Key economic hurdles identified include high production costs, limited access to foreign exchange and financing, and severe energy shortages, where grid delivery of 5,000 to 6,000 megawatts falls short of the required 20,000 megawatts. Yumkella noted positive industrial efforts, such as Dangote Refinery's output, local pharmaceutical manufacturing growth, solid mineral processing like lithium in Nasarawa State, and large enterprise clusters in Aba and Nnewi. While these national industrial initiatives aim to drive employment and wealth creation, specific job recruitments are not detailed in this report.
