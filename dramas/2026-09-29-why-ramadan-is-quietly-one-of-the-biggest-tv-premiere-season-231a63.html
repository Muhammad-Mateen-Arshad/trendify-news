Why Ramadan Is Quietly One of the Biggest TV Premiere Seasons on the Planet

Published by AI Pipeline | September 29, 2026

What's new

On the opening night of Ramadan, dozens of brand-new drama series debut within a single 24-hour period as the daily fast breaks. This intense launch window serves as the television industry's primary premiere season across the region, where a show's first-night ratings can determine a network's financial success for the entire year. Viewers can also watch these Arabic series online for free, expanding access beyond traditional broadcast schedules.

About the series

Known across the region as musalsalat, these serialized dramas run for all 30 days of Ramadan, delivering one episode each night until Eid. This traditional broadcast format originated with Egyptian state television in the 1960s before spreading across Syria, the Gulf, and the wider Arabic-speaking region. Today, major networks invest heavily in these productions, scheduling episodes twice daily: once during the evening meal after fast-breaking, and again before dawn during the early morning meal.

Regional industries bring distinct storytelling traditions to the season:

  • Egyptian productions: Focus frequently on family comedies and social satires addressing issues like class, divorce, and generational gaps.
  • Gulf dramas: Tend to be more formal, often highlighting business or royal families.
  • Syrian series: Famous for historical epics, with many creators later producing work through Gulf-funded studios.
  • Dubbed Turkish soap operas: Have maintained massive popularity for over ten years and compete directly against homegrown shows during this period.

What to expect

Streaming platforms have modernized how audiences engage with Ramadan television, allowing international viewers and diaspora communities to stream episodes on demand or follow along live with premiere nights.
