Netflix’s ‘Below’ Release Date, Time, Trailer, Cast, and Plot

Published on October 07, 2026

What's new

Netflix is expanding its spooky season lineup with 'Below', a six-episode limited thriller debuting on Thursday, October 8. Ahead of its streaming release, the show secured a high-profile spot as the opening night selection for the Primetime program at the Toronto International Film Festival. The platform has already shared key art alongside an official trailer, following earlier teasers in July that focused on psychological unease without revealing the monster. Specific episode runtimes are not specified in the source; please check the official notice.

The series will launch globally according to Netflix's standard schedule, arriving at midnight Pacific Time. Viewers in England can access the drop at 8 am British Summer Time, while audiences in Central and Eastern US regions will see it in the morning, followed by afternoon releases in India, South Korea, Japan, and eastern Australia.

About the series

Created and run by Jesse McKeown, 'Below' was filmed on location in St. John's, Newfoundland. The story centers on a small coastal community confronting a threat rising from the water. The primary cast includes:

  • Josh Hartnett as Calvin Penney, a stubborn fisherman still struggling with the mysterious death of his father decades earlier.
  • Mackenzie Davis as Fonda Howander, a visiting marine researcher.
  • Charlie Heaton as Wade Penney, Calvin's son.
  • Ruby Stokes as Mary, Rohan Campbell as Jay, and Willow Kean as Ruth, completing the Penney family.
  • Kaleb Horn as Noah, with Natasha Henstridge and Joshua Close in ensemble roles.

What to expect

The narrative blends suspense, local mythology, and humor as an unknown sea creature terrorizes the island. To protect his family and community, Calvin partners with Fonda to fight the entity and uncover hidden secrets. Alongside his lead role, Hartnett serves as an executive producer alongside Jessica Rhoades, Chris Hatcher, Jamie Childs, Louise Sutton, and Sharon Hall, delivering a story where the ocean itself acts as a primary antagonist.
