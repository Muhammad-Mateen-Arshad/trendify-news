New on Netflix October 2026: 5 best movies and shows to stream, plus full release list

Published by AI Pipeline | September 27, 2026

What's new

Netflix has revealed its content slate for October 2026, offering a diverse array of fresh titles and returning original programs for streaming fans. The upcoming schedule includes literary adaptations, tense thrillers, romantic dramas, and updated seasons of established favorites.

About the series

Several major television entries anchor the new lineup:

  • East of Eden: Debuting on October 2, this adaptation of John Steinbeck's 1955 book charts the history of the Trask and Hamilton families across multiple decades in California's Salinas Valley. Florence Pugh plays Cathy, a woman causing generational disruption, alongside co-stars Mike Faist, Christopher Abbott, and Ciarán Hinds.
  • Tyler Perry's Doing Life: Premiering on October 3, this drama written and directed by Tyler Perry centers on a former prison officer (Naomi Baker) who builds a connection with a reformed ex-convict (Jay Reeves) who previously saved her during a riot.
  • The Diplomat (Season 4): Arriving October 15, the political drama stars Keri Russell as Kate Wyler, Rufus Sewell as Hal, and Allison Janney as President Grace Penn. The story deals with the aftermath of a event that destabilizes international relations and worsens Kate's personal life.
  • Nobody Wants This (Season 3): Releasing September 22, the romantic series follows Joanne (Kristen Bell) and Noah (Adam Brody) as they decide to live together while Joanne prepares to convert to Judaism.

What to expect

On the film front, the crime thriller Animals arrives on October 9. Directed by Ben Affleck, who also stars alongside Kerry Washington, the movie follows an L.A. mayoral candidate forced to navigate a dark underground environment to save his abducted son.

Additionally, viewers can stream international projects including Brazil's The Gift of Deceit, South Africa's Crazy Rich, Incredibly Broke, and Japan's The Ramparts of Ice: Season 2.
