What's new

Submissions for the Academy Awards' Best International Feature Film category are approaching 90 entries ahead of the September 30 deadline. With 88 titles announced so far, the current lineup has already surpassed the 86 films that qualified last year. For the first time, a new qualification pathway allows movies that have secured awards across six designated festivals to enter the race, provided they satisfy all standard creative and release criteria.

About the entries

The current field features a wide array of international submissions, many of which debuted at the Cannes Film Festival in May. Notable submissions include:

  • Austria: "Rose," directed by Markus Schleinzer and starring Sandra Hüller as a 17th-century woman posing as a soldier to claim an estate.
  • Albania: "A State Film," a documentary by Roland Sejko constructed from Enver Hoxha regime propaganda footage without commentary.
  • Algeria: "Roqia," a horror thriller directed by Yanis Koussim exploring themes of exorcism and civil war history.
  • Argentina: "The Virgin of the Quarry Lake," a coming-of-age horror story set against the backdrop of the country's 2001 economic unrest.
  • Armenia: "In the Land of Arto," Tamara Stepanyan's narrative fiction debut starring Zar Amir Ebrahimi and Camille Cottin.
  • Australia: "First Light," a Filipino-language debut feature by James J. Robinson centered on a convent nun on Luzon island.

What to expect

Academy voters are expected to receive their lists of mandatory viewing titles in late October. The initial voting phase will take place from December 7 through December 11. Following the vote, a 15-film shortlist will be officially announced on December 15.
