Stories of individuals who reach advanced ages, such as 108-year-old Susan Young Browne who continues to live independently and stay active, frequently inspire questions about longevity. Research suggests that reaching age 100 is influenced by a combination of genetic, lifestyle, and social factors.

Key Takeaways

  • Research suggests daily lifestyle habits are linked to higher odds of reaching age 100 and continuing to live longer after turning 100.
  • Genetics is estimated to account for 20% to 40% of the variation in human lifespan, leaving a substantial role for daily habits and environmental factors.
  • Key habits associated with longevity include regular physical activity, avoiding smoking, consuming a varied diet, and maintaining strong social connections.

What the Source Says

A study published in JAMA Network Open followed over 5,000 Chinese adults aged 80 and older. Research suggests that individuals with the healthiest overall lifestyle scores were roughly 61% more likely to become centenarians compared to those with the least healthy habits. Specific habits independently associated with reaching 100 included never smoking (about 25% higher odds), active physical movement (about 31% higher odds), and eating a diverse range of food groups (about 23% higher odds). Moderate alcohol consumption and body mass index did not show strong associations with reaching 100 in this research.

Another study published in Maturitas tracked over 4,500 Chinese centenarians. Research suggests that maintaining healthy habits after age 100 continued to support longer survival. Centenarians with the most favorable habits—including diet variety, exercise, sleep quality, and non-smoking—lived about 11 months longer on average than those with unfavorable habits.

An editorial published in Nutrients reviewed the role of genetics and lifestyle, noting that genetics accounts for roughly 20% to 40% of longevity. The remaining variation relates to environment, diet, movement, and purpose. Data from the Longitudinal Ageing Study in India suggests that India's share of the global centenarian population could rise from 5.6% in 2000 to 8.3% by 2050.

Practical Notes

Physical activity, sleep quality, muscle strength, varied nutrition, and strong social ties support health and independence into older age.
